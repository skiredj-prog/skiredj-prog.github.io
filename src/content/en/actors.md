## A fragmented chain of responsibility

An AI action crosses a market: model, platform, data, integration, business rules and deployment decisions. Each actor knows its commercial perimeter; consequences cross the borders between them.

| Player | What they promise | What they should clarify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model vendor | Performance and autonomy | Version, limits and the boundary between model capability and system guarantee |
| Platform provider | Orchestration and integration | Services, permissions and responsibilities left to the customer |
| Integrator | Fast deployment | Configuration, connectors, tests and introduced assumptions |
| Customer organization | A reliable service or decision | Mandate, limits, supervision and reconstruction capability |
| Business team | An operational result | Rules, exceptions and decisions not implicitly delegated |
| DSI / security | Infrastructure and protection | Available controls, access, logs and effective stop conditions |
| Affected person | Receives the effect | The right to understand, challenge and obtain an answer |

## Five questions

1. What exactly is being promised?
2. What is actually controlled?
3. What is not controlled?
4. What evidence should exist?
5. Which responsibility may be sent elsewhere?

> **Distributed responsibility is not absent responsibility. It must be reconstructed before the incident, not invented after it.**
