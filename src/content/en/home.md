<p>TENIR 2.0 is a diagnostic and governance framework, reconstruction-first, for bounded units under pressure. It is concerned with admissibility, preservation of options, epistemic validity, recoverability, and posture. This page is a simplified public rendering. It follows KB3, the enacted and active doctrinal rendering line. KB3 renders meaning; it does not create sovereign meaning and is not a new constitution. It is a document layer, outside the code. Specialized services or applications wrap or extend it: they build on KB3 and must not redefine it. Definitions it does not restate are carried from KB2 (30 March 2026), the prior rendering line, which has no simultaneous active authority. The same word also names, in the repositories, an implementation: TENIR-Gov, the reference runtime of the Runtime Execution Governance (REG) standard. That implementation should align to KB3. It is not KB3.</p>

## Why this matters now

<p>Autonomous systems do more than produce answers. They call tools, send requests, change records, move money, delegate work, and trigger events outside themselves. Safety then becomes a question of execution: what must remain true while the system acts, and how to verify it when information is incomplete.</p>

<p>A component can enforce its local rule correctly and still leave the whole system in an inadmissible state. Two agents, each allowed to spend 1,000, can each execute one distinct payment and exceed a shared budget of 1,500. Idempotency stopped the duplicate. It did not hold the global limit.</p>

<p>A timeout is not proof of failure. A missing confirmation is not proof of success. The request may never have reached the service, or the service may have committed the effect and lost the acknowledgement. Retrying on silence alone can duplicate the effect. Fail-closed, here, does not mean giving up. It means not taking the next risky step while a necessary condition remains unproven.</p>

<blockquote>The model proposes. The constitutional layer adjudicates. The governance layer applies. The responsible human remains accountable.</blockquote>

## Three layers not to collapse

<div class="grid-3">
  <article class="card">
    <p class="role">Rendering · KB3</p>
    <h3>Rendered doctrine</h3>
    <p>TENIR 2.0 names the framework: admissible identity, gates, posture, collapse. KB3 is its enacted, active rendering line. It renders meaning; it is not a constitution, and it is not the code. KB2 is the prior rendering line. Governance and the machine source stay upstream.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card">
    <p class="role">Standard</p>
    <h3>REG</h3>
    <p>Runtime Execution Governance fixes the obligations of the execution boundary. RFC-0 through RFC-7 are frozen drafts. The standard does not prescribe the mechanism. An execution disposition is not a posture.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="card">
    <p class="role">Platform</p>
    <h3>TENIR-Gov</h3>
    <p>The reference implementation, the IronOmega line and then 5.x. It should align to KB3. It is not KB3. 5.6.1 is not a publication.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<p>The public guide <em>Beyond the Layers</em> states the distinction for a non-specialist reader: governance defines what is allowed; middleware and runtime enforce constraints at the moment of action. Architecture diagrams show where components live. They do not say what those components protect. That guide is a simplification. It is not the rendering line.</p>
